International Transport to Brno

Airports

Brno is close to several major airports, with the 4 most significant options listed below.
  • Brno airport (IATA: BRQ, aerial distance 9 km from the venue).

    The city has two airports, a small one and a tiny one. The international airport offers regular flights to

    • Milan/Bergamo (Italy),
    • London/Stansted (UK),
    • Málaga (Spain).
    In addition, a few more routes to summer destinations (mainly in Greece, Egypt and Turkey) are still operated in October.

  • Vienna airport (IATA: VIE, aerial distance 121 km).
    • The capital of Austria provides the most convenient large hub for travels to Brno.
    • Both direct and indirect buses and trains of multiple companies operate between Vienna airport and Brno (see below).
    • The journey to Brno takes around 2 hours and 15 minutes.
  • Bratislava airport (IATA: BTS, aerial distance 124 km).
    • The airport in the Slovak capital can be an alternative to Vienna Airport. It offers a good range of connections.
    • Still, the traffic is very limited, and consequently, the journey to Brno can be complicated. The best way, if possible, requires a single change at Bratislava's main bus station. Brno residents usually do not use it.
    • The journey to Brno can take around 2 hours and 30 minutes.
  • Prague airport (IATA: PRG, aerial distance 196 km).
    • The airport in the Czech capital is located in the north-west of the city, i.e. the opposite side from Brno.
    • Some buses run directly between Prague Airport and Brno. Passing through Prague using local public transport is time-consuming and complicated.
    • Note in particular that bus AE (Airport Express) in Prague is 4 times more expensive (200 CZK) than ordinary public transport, e.g., trolleybus 59, buses 100 and 324, and underground (up to 50 CZK), while not significantly faster. At Prague Airport, Terminal 1 serves non-Schengen flights, while Terminal 2 serves Schengen flights.
    • The journey to Brno can take around 2 hours and 30 minutes. Still, time to time, the delays are significant.

Inter-city trains and buses

Many companies in Central Europe operate international buses and trains.
  • The drawback is the typical incompatibility of their systems and tickets, which includes traps.
  • Three different companies ‒ Czech Railways, RegioJet and Austrian ÖBB ‒ label their connections as RJ<NUMBER>, but a ticket purchased from one company is not necessarily valid for a connection operated by another company.
  • In most of the cases, tickets issued by Czech Railways and RegioJet are not interchangeable. RegioJet usually uses lowercase rj, while Czech Railways and ÖBB use uppercase RJ.
  • To avoid issues, pay attention to how you purchase your ticket and to the colour of the vehicle. International companies usually run their own websites and mobile apps, where one can find connections and buy tickets directly to be sure about the vehicle's colour to look for.
  • Note: The conditions require passengers to be on the platform at least 15 minutes before departure, but this is not often obeyed. Also, the later you buy a ticket for an international journey, the more expensive it usually becomes. If multiple companies operate the same route, their connections usually interleave, so to safe time, tt might be a good idea to check them all.

Some of the most important companies are:

  • RegioJet (website + eshop, Android app, iOS app)

    Czech company founded in Brno in 2009 is one of Europe’s largest long-distance bus and train operators. It provides rail and bus transport across around 15 countries.

    • Direct buses often run between Vienna Airport and Brno. The bus stop is right in front of the terminal, and buses to Brno almost always use Platform 2.
    • Rarely, direct RegioJet trains go from the Vienna airport to Brno, so check whether your connection is a train or a bus.
    • There are a few direct bus connections between Brno and Prague airport. Their connections with a single change in Prague’s city centre (Florenc) are also usually better than alternatives.
    • Alert: RegioJet trains operate, among others, the Budapest–Vienna Hbf–Brno(–Prague) route. They never operate on the Graz–Vienna Hbf–Brno(-Prague) route and their tickets are not valid on any train running there.

    Their buses and trains are typically yellow. In case of emergency, bus can be also white.

  • Flix Bus (website + eshop, Android app, iOS app)

    While Germany company FlixBus also operates trains, they run only buses in Czechia.

    • Direct buses often run between Vienna Airport and Brno. The bus stop is right in front of the terminal, and buses to Brno almost always use Platform 2.
    • There are a few direct bus connections between Brno and Prague airport. Their connections with a single change in Prague’s city centre (Florenc) are also usually better than alternatives.
    • Alert: Timetables often claim that FlixBus is much faster than RegioJet on the same route, but RegioJet is often ahead of schedule, while FlixBus tends to be on time or late.

    Their buses and trains are typically green. If it is not, the bus wears a large Flix logo.

  • Czech Railways (website + eshop, Android app, iOS app)

    The state-owned national railway company operates most routes in the Czech Republic, and many international services, including:

    • some of the trains to Austria, including Vienna – usually Hbf only, not the airport,
    • Prague – many stations including the Main Train Station (Hlavní nádraží),
    • Slovakia, including Bratislava.
    • They also operate direct trains between Prague and Copenhagen (Denmark) via Berlin, Hamburg, Kolding and Odense.
    • Alert: A ticket from Czech Railways (e.g. Brno to Vienna Airport) may cover multiple segments, including those run by foreign operators completely in the foreign country (e.g. Austrian ÖBB between Vienna Hbf and airport). However, buying the foreign segment through Czech Railways is several times more expensive than buying it directly from the foreign operator. A single ticket bought at an ÖBB machine costs ~€4.50 for the Hbf–airport journey in Vienna.
    Typically, the Czech Railways' vagoons are blue.
  • ÖBB: Trains between Vienna Main Train Station and Brno can also be operated by red trains of the austrian ÖBB. Since they operate in Czechia, technically Czech Railways bring responsibility and sell tickets for these (for common prices) in shops listed above without distinguishing the trains.
  • Besides the Google maps, the most of the connections going to/within Czechia can be found in IDOS. It usually allows buying tickets as well, but international tickets are usually very expensive in this app. The local tickets are ok.

Transport in Brno

There’s a good chance you won’t need public transport in Brno.

  • International trains always arrive at the Main Train Station (Hlavní nádraží) in Brno, founded in 1839 as part of the first steam railway in the Austrian Empire. It serves as the largest hub of a public transport in the city.
  • International buses typically arrive to Bus Station Grand Hotel which is a 280-metre walk from the Main Train Station transport hub.

    If you want to use trams 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 10, or 12, use the underpass and find the correct platform for your line on the screen inside.

    To use other lines (including bus 67), you do not need to use the underpass.

  • Hotel Continental is 1.3 km far from the Main Train Station. The night buses going nearby are N92, N93, and N99. Also, N95 goes close.
  • Hotel Cosmopolitan (Boby Center) is 2.8 km far from the Main Train Station. The nearby night bus stop is Hrnčířská, served by routes N90 and N91. The stop is close to the conference venue.

    During the day, you can take bus 67 directly from Brno Main Railway Station to the hotel. Get off at Za Lužánkami (towards Jundrov, the final destination). The bus stop at the main station is located here.

  • From Brno airport:

    Trolleybus 21 and bus 77 operate between Brno Airport and the city center between ~5:00 and ~22:30. The night bus is N89.

Public transport in Brno

Brno provides a very dense public transport network operated by DPMB. It serves the entire city and some surrounding villages 24 hours a day, every day of the year. Many people consider it one of the best urban transport systems in the world. However, it can be somewhat complicated for foreigners to navigate.

  • The whole city is covered by zones 100 and 101. The tickets always cover both
  • Lines 1–12 are trams,
  • Lines 21–39 are trolleybuses,
  • Lines 40–84 are busses. Letter E denotes the fast (express) line (e.g. E75) that skips some of the stops. Letter x denotes replacement service, typically with a differet route.
  • Lines N89–N99 are night buses.
    • They operating from ~22:30 to ~5:30 before working days and from ~22:30 to ~7:30 on other days.
    • All 22 buses wait for each other (but be quick!) in front of the Main Train Station (Hlavní nádraží) and depart every day at 23:00, 23:30, 0:00, 0:30, 1:00, 2:00, 3:00, 4:00, 4:30, and 5:00 to cover the whole city.
    • The Main Train Station (Hlavní nádraží) is located approximately halfway along the route of every night bus line. You can take any night bus to get to the Main Trani Station and transfer to your desired bus. The only thing to watch out for is the direction of travel.
  • Lines 100–961 are regional buses. (Not all of them go to Brno.) If you do not need to use them, we advise avoiding them, as the ticketing system is slightly different.
  • Most stops are request stops (except major transport hubs). If you want to get off, press the button next to the door in advance (not the one on the opposite side of the vehicle). If you want to board, wait visibly at the stop; otherwise, the driver may not stop. You usually need to open the doors yourself by pressing the door button.

Tickets

  • Option 1 is the simplest:
    • tap your contactless credit or debit card on a validator inside the vehicle. You can switch the validator into English. While it offers multiple choices, you do not need to change anything if you want a single adult ticket for a travel in the city.
    • After taping, check the light and sound: if the light is red and you hear an error tone, try again. The green colour with a friendly tone denotes success.
    • There are several validators in each vehicle. You need a valid ticket even if some of the validators is out of service.
    • Do not forget to check-in your again at each entrance to each vehicle.
    • Only voluntarily, you can check-out yourself in the same way when leaving the vehicle. This can bring a discount only if you spent up to 15 minutes in a single vehicle.
    • The money is charged a day after your travel(s), and the amount is optimised: The systems and covers all journeys within the previous day (0:00–23:59) with tickets to minimise the amount paid!
    • You are gueranteed to pay up to 99 CZK per day (~€4). A single journey up to 60 minutes (including changes) costs 25 CZK (~€1), and a short trip up to 15 minutes costs 20 CZK (~€0.8).
    • Note that no physical confirmation (paper ticket) is issued. Your credit card will only be checked if the inspector requests verification.
  • Option 2: If you want to have your tickets in advance, use, e.g., the IDOS app (Android, iOS).
    • Here, you need to store your credit card (preferably in advance),
    • then find a specific connection and buy a ticket for it.
    • This ticket has to be bought in advance. It does not work a minutes before the departure.
    • While the app enables you to buy a ticket via SMS, this requires a Czech phone number.
  • Option 3: Paper tickets can be bought on streets in (typically) yellow machines (see their map here).
    • The most of them accepts Czech coins only,
    • Very few machines accepts credit card payments (here on the left).
    • All machines on the train platforms of the Main train station accepts credit cards.

Miscellaneous

  • The most common supermarket brands in the Czech Republic are: Albert , Brněnka , Billa , Lidl , and various local shops.

    While rare, some Brněnka supermarkets and other local shops do not accept payment cards. International supermarket chains such as Albert, Billa, and Lidl always accept cards.

  • English proficiency among Czechs can be initially inferred from their age.
  • Many fast-food restaurants operate in Brno. We advise against KFC.
  • Service providers in Brno usually accept payment cards, but exceptions do exist. It is advisable to check this in advance.
  • Service providers usually accept credit and debit cards, but there are exceptions. It is advisable to check this in advance.
  • If you need Czech koruna in cash, we recommend using official ATMs operated by Czech banks, such as Komerční banka KB, ČSOB, Česká spořitelna, and MONETA. We strongly recommend avoiding Euronet ATMs. In general, ATMs at airports can be problematic.
